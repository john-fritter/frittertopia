# Session notes — 2026-04-17

First real play session. One player (testuserA), walked all six rooms, abused the look
command extensively. Notes are honest.

---

## What works

**Fog arrival sequence.** Three beats, well-paced, strange and quiet. Sets the tone
exactly right. The line "as if someone else had been doing it for you" is good. Don't
touch this.

**First-visit room descriptions.** The LLM is doing strong work with the authored briefs.
Courtyard, corridor, dormitory, chapel, kitchen, herb garden — all held up. Rich without
being overwrought. The `[bracketed]` detail sections in the briefs are pulling their
weight: `look well`, `look altar`, `look linen cloth`, `look bundles` all returned
coherent, specific detail grounded in the authored text.

**Weird look targets handled gracefully (mostly).** `look at the smell`, `look at my
skirt`, `look at what I have done with my life` all stayed in-world without crashing.
`look at a million cots` correctly counted eight. The LLM is resilient to player
nonsense more often than not.

**Tone is landing.** Lonely, concrete, dreamlike. The writing is the art direction and it
shows — the whole session read like the project is supposed to feel.

**Exit labels.** Showing destinations in parentheses (`west (The Monastery Courtyard)`)
is a small thing that matters a lot. Keep it.

---

## What doesn't work

**The LLM hallucinates objects.** This is the biggest problem.

- `look potato` (kitchen) → LLM described picking up a potato from a wooden bowl. No
  potato exists.
- `look earthworm` (kitchen) → LLM described a worm on the stone floor. No worm exists.
- `look shadow` (kitchen) → LLM described a fleeting dark shape. No shadow entity exists.
- `look at the stone broom` → LLM described a stone broom in careful detail. The broom
  is wood.

The world becomes untrustworthy. Players will learn they can `look` things into existence.
This needs fixing before more people play.

**The LLM breaks character on refusals.** Two clear examples:

- `look at the burning blanket` → *"I apologize, but I cannot describe a burning blanket
  in this context."* First person. Meta. Immersion destroyed.
- `look like a cool person` → LLM gave meta-instructions: "try examining the hearth,
  table, herbs..."

The LLM has no persona instruction for graceful in-world refusals. It falls back to
assistant voice. This needs a prompt fix.

**Revisit stubs are jarring.** Moving west from the corridor back to the courtyard showed:

```
a crumbling stone courtyard
```

Moving back to the corridor showed:

```
a long stone corridor
```

The `Description.short` stubs are fine as a technical fallback. They're terrible as a
player experience. The drop from a rich LLM description to a single flat line — on the
same room you were just in — feels like the game broke. Players who don't know to type
`look` will think something went wrong.

**Player presence is flat.** "root is here." No texture, no flavor. And `look root`
was interpreted as looking for vegetation (reasonable, but wrong). There's no handler
that recognizes a player name as a look target.

**Numeric look edge cases are inconsistent.** `look 7 times` was interpreted as the 7th
cot (forgivable). `look at the 587th cot` — the LLM described it as if it existed, with
a pristine folded blanket and everything. There are eight cots. `look at a million cots`
correctly pushed back. The LLM has no concept of actual room contents to check against.

---

## Suggestions

**1. Ground the LLM with what's actually in the room.** Pass the explicit list of
entities and items present to the target-look prompt. If the look target doesn't match
any entity and isn't mentioned in the `RoomBrief`, don't hit the LLM at all — return a
terse in-world line and be done. Something like: *"You don't see anything like that
here."* This is the highest-value fix, and it's a prompt + resolver change, not an
architecture change.

**2. Add a persona instruction for refusals.** The LLM prompt should tell the model:
when a target doesn't exist, when a request is out of scope, or when describing something
would contradict the room, respond in the narrator's voice, not as an assistant. No
apologies, no first person, no meta-instructions. Just a quiet in-world acknowledgment.

**3. Fix the revisit experience.** Two options worth considering:

- Show the short stub but append a soft cue: *"(type `look` to take in the room)"*.
  Low cost, solves the confusion.
- Use a shorter cached LLM summary on revisit — one or two sentences drawn from the
  brief. Higher cost, better experience.

Option (a) is probably the right call for now.

**4. Player presence and `look [player]`.** `Player` entities should have a
`Presence.description` with actual flavor — something that fits the tone. `look [player]`
should be handled as a distinct case in the resolver, not delegated to the room LLM.

**5. `take` and `drop`.** The most conspicuous absence. The blanket, the broom, the
candle — players will try to pick things up. Items feel inert without it. This is the
next natural layer of interaction.

---

## Overall

The foundation is solid. The writing quality and LLM coherence are better than expected
for a first session. The main weakness is that the LLM has no grounding in what's
actually real in a given room — it will describe anything plausible. Fix that and the
world will feel trustworthy. Everything else is polish.
