Create an internal physical continuity record for a player character.

The record is never shown directly to the player. It will be given to the live storyteller so the character can be described consistently when relevant.

Use only the provided CharacterRoll or player-written source description.

For rolled characters:
- Preserve all rolled traits exactly.
- Do not add scars, marks, tattoos, injuries, callouses, jewelry, clothing, equipment, occupation, social status, culture, personality, morality, memories, or backstory.
- Do not infer anything from gender except pronouns and whether facial hair is plausible.

For freeform player descriptions:
- Preserve explicitly stated physical facts as source truth.
- Anything not specified may vary between incarnations.
- If the description violates playability constraints, reinterpret it as the nearest playable humanoid version and include an ADJUSTMENT note.

All player characters are mortal, land-walking, breathing, eating, drinking, speaking, bipedal humanoids. They can fit through a normal doorway, wear clothing, carry a pack, use tools, and die.

Do not describe the character as unremarkable, forgettable, ugly, beautiful, monstrous, divine, cursed, sick, weak, lazy, disciplined, poor, wealthy, dangerous, harmless, noble, evil, holy, or doomed unless explicitly supplied by the player.

Do not assign race, species, ancestry, culture, origin, curse, blessing, or destiny. Strange bodies are allowed to remain unexplained.

Output format:

CANON:
- Always include this section.
- Use as few bullets as needed. Ordinary characters may need only two.
- List stable physical facts. Preserve all rolled traits exactly.

RENDERING NOTES:
- Optional. Include only if the character has traits that are unusual, easy to misuse, or have specific physical consequences the storyteller might miss.
- Do not include notes about ordinary skin tones, common hair colors, ordinary eye colors, typical height, or typical build. The storyteller does not need to be told how to describe brown hair or a tall person.
- Good rendering notes describe when a trait becomes visible, tactile, audible, inconvenient, concealed, or affected by weather, light, movement, clothing, touch, water, cold, doorways, or close inspection.
- Bad rendering notes explain obvious facts, infer personality, create abilities, or repeat global rules.
- Do not invent behavior. Do not say a character shivers sooner, tires faster, seeks warmth, moves carefully, is capable, is agile, is strong, or is weak unless explicitly supplied by the source.

AVOID:
- Optional. Include only if this specific character has traits likely to trigger bad inference.
- Be specific to this character. Do not repeat global rules.
- Use AVOID to prevent likely mistakes: treating white eyes as blindness, fur as animal nature, horns as demonic, webbed fingers as aquatic ancestry, tattoos as rank or culture, age as wisdom or frailty, claws as predatory morality, luminous skin as divine or cursed, absent ears as deafness.

ADJUSTMENT:
- Optional. Omit unless traits had to be changed to make the character playable. One sentence if included.

Examples of correct brief length:

Ordinary character (male, middle-aged, average height, lean build, fair skin, dark brown eyes, grey hair, no fantastical features, no marks):
CANON:
- Male, middle-aged, average height, lean build.
- Fair skin, dark brown eyes, grey hair.
[No further sections needed.]

Unusual character (male teen, short, gaunt, furred skin, green eyes, bald, digitigrade legs, no marks):
CANON:
- Male teen, short stature, gaunt build.
- Furred skin, green eyes, bald head.
- Digitigrade legs.

RENDERING NOTES:
- Digitigrade legs affect walking gait, balance, footwear, and tracks on soft ground.
- Fur changes visibly with rain, dust, cold, and close inspection.

AVOID:
- Do not frame fur or digitigrade legs as animalistic, monstrous, or proof of species or origin.
- Do not assume special agility, speed, or climbing ability unless game state grants it.
